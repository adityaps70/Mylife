Snowball or avalanche: which should you pick?

Avalanche always costs the least interest, because the most expensive debt shrinks first. Snowball clears small debts early, and in a study of real debt-repayment accounts, people who closed out small balances first were more likely to clear all their debt (Gal and McShane, Journal of Marketing Research, 2012). The sheet shows the rupee cost of each, so you can decide whether quick wins are worth it for you. In India the two often agree, because a credit card is frequently both the smallest balance and the highest rate.

How to use it

  1. Type what you can put towards debts each month in the first lime cell. Work it out after rent, food, bills and savings for emergencies; the monthly budget sheet helps.
  2. Overwrite the three example debts. For a loan, use the outstanding principal and the EMI from your latest loan statement (or the free EMI tracker sheet, which works it out). For a card, use the total outstanding and the minimum due on your statement.
  3. Read the comparison table at the top, then open the Avalanche or Snowball tab to see the month-by-month plan.
  4. Each month, update the balances from your statements. The plan resets from today’s numbers.
  5. Google Sheets: File → Import → Upload. The formulas and drop-downs carry over.

The sheet adds interest monthly at the yearly rate ÷ 12. Card issuers charge interest daily, add 18% GST on it, and lower the minimum due as the balance falls, so treat card figures as estimates; the credit card interest calculator shows the real cost of paying only the minimum. Before prepaying a fixed-rate loan, check your sanction letter for a prepayment charge.

Questions

Is this debt snowball spreadsheet free?

Yes. Download it without signing up or giving an email, and use it for up to 10 debts.

Does it do the debt avalanche method too?

Yes. The same file works out both methods and a minimums-only plan from one list of debts, and shows the debt-free month and total interest for each.

What if I can’t afford all my minimum payments?

The sheet shows a red warning and the plans still pay every minimum, so the shortfall is clear. If your EMIs and minimums are more than you can pay, talk to your lender about restructuring before you miss a payment.

Does it work in rupees?

Yes. Amounts are in rupees with Indian digit grouping (₹1,50,000), and the debt types are the ones Indian households have: cards, personal loans, EMIs, gold loans and pay-later.
