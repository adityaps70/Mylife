Why the minimum due is so expensive

The minimum due keeps your account in good standing, but it isn’t a plan to pay the bill. On a typical Indian card, interest of about 3.5% a month is close to 42% a year before GST. When you pay only the minimum, most of that payment goes to interest and very little reduces what you owe.

There’s a second cost that the calculator doesn’t show: while a balance is carried, most cards charge interest on new purchases from the day you make them, so the interest-free period disappears until the bill is cleared in full.

Three ways out

  • Pay a fixed amount well above the minimum every month. Try it in the calculator.
  • Clear the card before other debts. Card interest is usually the most expensive debt you have; see debt avalanche vs snowball.
  • Consider a cheaper loan to clear it, such as a personal loan at a lower rate, but only if you stop adding to the card. Compare with the personal loan EMI calculator.

Know the dates

Interest only starts when the full bill isn’t paid by the due date. Read statement date vs due date for how the interest-free period works, and let the credit card bill reminder warn you before every due date.

Questions

How is credit card interest calculated in India?

If you don’t pay the full bill by the due date, most cards charge interest every month on the unpaid balance, typically around 3–3.75% a month, plus 18% GST on that interest. Most cards also stop giving an interest-free period on new spends until the old balance is cleared.

What happens if I pay only the minimum due?

You avoid a late fee, but the rest of the bill carries interest. Because the minimum is small (often 5% of the bill), most of each payment goes to interest and the balance falls very slowly.

Is this calculator exact?

It is an estimate. Banks calculate interest daily from each transaction date and may add fees, so your statement will differ slightly. The calculator assumes no new spending and a minimum due of at least ₹200.
