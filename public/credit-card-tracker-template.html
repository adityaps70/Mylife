Why the statement day matters more than the due day

Take a card with a statement on the 4th and payment due on the 24th (the example HDFC Bank uses in its own terms). Buy something on 4 December and it goes on that day’s bill, due 24 December: 20 days to pay. Buy it on 5 December and it goes on the January bill, due 24 January: 50 days. Same card, same amount, one day apart. The tracker shows this number for every card each day, so you can pick the card that gives you the most time before a big purchase.

The interest-free period only applies if you paid the previous bill in full. Pay less and interest is charged from the date of each purchase; see the credit card interest calculator for what paying only the minimum costs. RBI rules require card issuers to give you at least a fortnight to pay after the bill is sent (RBI Master Direction on credit cards, 2022). How to use it

  1. Overwrite the three example cards. The statement date and payment due date are printed at the top of every card statement; type just the day of the month.
  2. Add purchases to the Spends tab as you go, or once a week from your card app. Pick the card from the drop-down.
  3. When you pay a bill in full, set “Last bill paid?” to Yes. When the next statement arrives, set it back to No.
  4. Check the “Last bill” column against your statement. If it differs, a spend was posted a day late or you missed one; your statement is always right.
  5. Google Sheets: File → Import → Upload. The formulas, drop-downs and colours carry over.

Questions

Is this credit card tracker Excel sheet free?

Yes. Download it without signing up or giving an email, and use it for up to 10 cards and 500 purchases.

Do I need to enter my card number?

No. The sheet only needs a name for each card, the bank, the credit limit, the statement day and the due day. Never type a full card number or CVV into any spreadsheet.

How does it know which bill a purchase goes on?

A purchase made on or before the statement day goes on that statement; anything after it rolls to the next one. Banks sometimes post a spend a day or two late, so a purchase on the statement day itself can land on either bill. Your statement is the final word.

What if my statement date is the 30th or 31st?

Type 30 or 31. In shorter months the sheet uses the last day of the month, which is what most banks do. Check your statement for February.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. In Google Sheets choose File, Import, Upload and pick the file. The formulas, drop-downs and colours keep working.
