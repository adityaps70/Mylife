Debt avalanche vs snowball: which pays off debt faster?

Both methods pay the minimum on every debt and put any extra money towards one debt at a time. They differ only in which debt gets the extra first, and that choice changes how much interest you pay and how motivated you stay.

By Aditya Pratap Singh · Published 2026-11-13 · Updated 2026-11-13 · 4 min read

Short answer: The avalanche method puts extra money on the highest-interest debt first and costs the least interest. The snowball method clears the smallest balance first for quick wins. In our example below, avalanche saves about ₹1,645 more than snowball, while both are debt-free in 13 months.

What is the debt avalanche method?

The debt avalanche method pays the minimum on every debt, then puts every extra rupee towards the debt with the highest interest rate. When that debt is gone, its payment rolls on to the next-highest rate. Because the most expensive debt shrinks first, avalanche always costs the least total interest.

In India the highest rate is almost always a credit card balance (about 36–45% a year), followed by personal loans, consumer durable EMIs, car loans and home loans.

What is the debt snowball method?

The debt snowball method pays the minimum on every debt, then puts every extra rupee towards the smallest balance, whatever its rate. Each cleared debt frees up its payment for the next one, so the amount you put towards debt grows like a rolling snowball. You get early wins, which helps many people stick with the plan.

Avalanche vs snowball: a worked example

Three debts and ₹20,000 a month to put towards them in total:

Minimums add up to ₹10,000, so ₹10,000 a month is extra. Simulating month by month, with interest added monthly:

Avalanche13 months₹23,584Card and phone (month 6), loan (month 13)

Two lessons stand out. First, avalanche saves money (₹1,645 here), but the gap is small when balances are modest. Second, the big win is paying extra at all: either plan saves over ₹41,000 and 17 months compared with paying only the minimums.

What the research says about motivation

A 2012 study in the Journal of Marketing Research by David Gal and Blakeley McShane looked at people in a debt settlement programme. People who closed off individual debt accounts were more likely to clear all their debt, independent of the amount they had repaid. Small victories seem to help people keep going.

That is the case for snowball: if seeing a debt disappear in month 2 keeps you on the plan, the extra interest can be worth it.

Where the extra money comes from

Both methods depend on paying more than the minimums. Common places to find it:

  • Cut wants for a few months: food delivery, subscriptions, shopping. Even ₹3,000–5,000 a month speeds things up.
  • Put bonuses, tax refunds and gifts straight towards the first debt in your list.
  • Sell unused items, or take on short-term extra work, and use all of it for debt.
  • When a debt is cleared, keep paying the same total. Do not let lifestyle absorb the freed EMI.

Which method should you choose?

  • Choose avalanche if you have a high-interest credit card balance, if the balances are large, or if you are motivated by numbers.
  • Choose snowball if you have several small debts, if you have tried and given up before, or if quick wins keep you going.
  • Hybrid: clear any tiny balance first for a quick win, then switch to avalanche.

Whichever you choose, stop adding new debt to the cards you are paying off, and keep a small emergency fund so one surprise bill does not undo your progress.

If the minimums alone are too much

If minimum payments and EMIs already take more than your income can bear, neither method works yet. Steps to consider:

  • Talk to your lender early, before you miss a payment. Ask about restructuring, a longer tenure or converting a card balance into an EMI at a lower rate.
  • Consolidate expensive debt. A personal loan or balance transfer at a lower rate can replace card interest, but only if you stop using the card.
  • Never pay one loan with another expensive loan, such as instant loan apps, which often charge very high fees.
  • Get help. Banks have grievance and customer service channels, and RBI’s complaint system is there if a lender does not respond.

How to make your own plan

  1. List every debt with its balance, interest rate and minimum payment.
  2. Work out how much you can put towards debt each month after essentials.
  3. Pick avalanche or snowball and order the list.
  4. Pay minimums on all, and the extra on the first debt in your order.
  5. When a debt is cleared, add its payment to the next one.

Common questions

Should I take a personal loan to pay off a credit card?

It can save a lot, since personal loans usually cost far less than card interest. It only helps if you stop adding new spending to the card.

Should I prepay my home loan before other debts?

Usually not. Home loans have the lowest rates; clear credit cards and personal loans first.
