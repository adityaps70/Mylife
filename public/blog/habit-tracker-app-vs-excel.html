Habit tracker app vs Excel sheet vs paper: which actually works?

A habit tracker only helps if you keep using it. The three common choices, an app, a spreadsheet and a paper journal, fail in different ways, so the right one depends on how you tend to drop off.

By Aditya Pratap Singh · Published 2026-11-15 · Updated 2026-11-15 · 4 min read

Short answer: Use a paper journal if you like a daily pen-and-paper ritual and track a few habits. Use an Excel or Google Sheets template if you want free, flexible tracking and review on a laptop. Use an app if you have quit trackers before because you forgot: reminders at the right time and one-tap logging are what keep most people going.

Why most habit trackers get abandoned

People rarely stop tracking because the tracker was badly designed. They stop because, on a busy day, nothing put the habit in front of them, then one missed day became three, and the sheet or app started to feel like a record of failure.

Two findings from habit research explain what a good tracker has to do. In a 2010 University College London study, new daily habits took a median of 66 days to feel automatic, with a range of 18 to 254 days, and missing a single day did not meaningfully set people back. So a tracker has to survive about two months of use, including a few missed days. And a meta-analysis of 94 independent tests found that deciding exactly when and where you will act (“after morning chai, I’ll read 10 pages”) has a medium-to-large effect on actually doing it. A tracker that ties each habit to a time helps with that.

Paper habit journal

A printed grid or a bullet-journal page: one row per habit, one box per day.

  • Good at: it’s always visible on your desk, ticking a box with a pen feels satisfying, and there’s no screen involved.
  • Weak at: no reminders, no totals or streaks unless you count them yourself, and it stays at home when you travel.
  • Best for: two or three daily habits, done at home, by someone who already journals.

Excel or Google Sheets template

A spreadsheet with a tab per month and a cell per day. A good template counts your days done, completion rate and best streak with formulas. You can download our free habit tracker template to try it.

  • Good at: free, completely flexible, your data stays in a file you own, and it’s great for a weekly review on a laptop.
  • Weak at: no reminders, and logging on a phone means opening a file and tapping a tiny cell. Most people who drop spreadsheets do it because daily entry on mobile is fiddly.
  • Best for: people who like data, plan on a laptop, and are disciplined about opening the sheet every day.

Habit tracker app

An app on your phone that knows each habit’s schedule.

  • Good at: a notification at the time you planned, one-tap logging, streaks and charts that update themselves, and habits that aren’t daily (three times a week, monthly).
  • Weak at: notifications become noise if you add too many habits, some apps lock basic features behind a subscription, and your data lives in the app.
  • Best for: anyone who has started a tracker before and stopped after a week or two.

How to choose in one minute

  1. Have you used a tracker before and stopped? If yes, the problem was remembering. Pick an app with reminders.
  2. Will you log on your phone most days? If yes, an app; if you’re on a laptop daily anyway, a spreadsheet is fine.
  3. Tracking only two or three habits at home? Paper is enough and has no setup.
  4. Want money, water or protein next to your habits? A spreadsheet can do it with extra tabs; an app like Self Streak does it in one place.

Whatever you choose, start with two or three habits, tie each one to something you already do every day, and don’t let one missed day turn into two.

Common questions

Is a habit tracker app better than Excel?

For most people, yes, because of reminders and one-tap logging on the phone. Excel is better if you want a free file you control and you review on a laptop.

Can I use Google Sheets as a habit tracker?

Yes. Import an Excel template into Google Sheets (File, Import, Upload) and it keeps the formulas. Mark each day with a 1 and the sheet counts your streak.

How many habits should I track?

Start with two or three. Add one more only after the first ones happen without effort, which for many people takes around two months.
