What’s in the free budget sheet

  • Budget tab with rupee categories that fit Indian households: rent or home loan EMI, groceries, electricity, mobile, other EMIs, fuel, school fees, Swiggy/Zomato, OTT, festivals, SIPs, emergency fund and PPF.
  • 50/30/20 check built in: it compares your needs, wants and savings with 50%, 30% and 20% of your take-home pay.
  • Expenses tab: add each spend with a date, a category from a drop-down and how you paid (UPI, card, cash). The Budget tab adds them up and turns a category red when it goes over.
  • Sample figures for a ₹75,000 salary, so you can see how it works before typing yours.

How to use it

  1. Type your take-home salary in the lime cell, then a budget for each category.
  2. Every day or two, add what you spent to the Expenses tab. Pick the category from the list.
  3. Check the summary at the top: if “Wants” is over its target, that’s where to cut first.
  4. Start a fresh copy each month (File → Save as), so each month keeps its own record.

Questions

Is this budget template free?

Yes, free to download and use, with no signup or email.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. In Google Sheets choose File, Import, Upload and pick the file. The drop-downs and totals keep working.

Which categories does it use?

Needs (rent or home loan, groceries, utilities, mobile, EMIs, transport, insurance, fees, medicines), wants (eating out, shopping, outings, subscriptions, travel, festivals) and savings (SIPs, emergency fund, PPF or EPF top-up, extra loan payments). Rename any of them.

Can I use it for a family budget?

Yes. Enter the household’s combined take-home pay and add every member’s spends to the Expenses tab.
