What’s in the free EMI tracker

Worked example: what a ₹2 lakh prepayment does

On the example home loan (₹40 lakh, 8.5%, 20 years), the schedule shows total interest of ₹43,31,065 over 240 EMIs. Type ₹2,00,000 into the extra-payment column at EMI number 12 and keep the EMI the same: the loan closes in 214 EMIs instead of 240, and total interest falls to ₹36,17,959, ₹7,13,106 less. The earlier in the loan you prepay, the bigger the saving, because more of each early EMI is interest.

Before prepaying, check your sanction letter. Floating-rate home loans to individuals usually have no prepayment charge; many fixed-rate car and personal loans do. The home loan EMI calculator and car loan EMI calculator explain the rules for each.

How to use it

  1. Overwrite the three example rows with your loans. Take the amount, rate, tenure and first EMI date from your sanction letter or loan statement.
  2. Check that the EMI column matches what your bank deducts. A difference of a few rupees is rounding. A bigger gap usually means the rate changed (floating loans) or the bank charged broken-period interest; in that case, start a new row from today with the outstanding amount, rate and months left from your latest statement.
  3. Type your take-home salary at the top to see what share of it goes to EMIs.
  4. Open the Schedule tab, pick a loan, and try extra payments to see which loan to prepay first. The debt avalanche vs snowball guide explains the two usual orders.
  5. Google Sheets: File → Import → Upload. The formulas and drop-downs carry over.

Questions

Is this EMI tracker Excel sheet free?

Yes. Download it without signing up or giving an email, and use it for as many loans as you have (up to 30 rows).

How does it calculate the outstanding loan balance?

It counts the EMIs due between your first EMI date and today, then works out the balance left on a reducing-balance loan after that many EMIs. That is the same method banks use, so it should be within a few rupees of your statement unless the rate changed.

Does it work for no-cost EMI and credit card EMI?

Yes. Enter 0% as the rate for a no-cost EMI and the card’s EMI rate for a credit card EMI. Note that banks may still charge a processing fee and GST on the interest of a card EMI, which the sheet does not add.

Can I track a floating-rate home loan?

Yes, but when the rate changes, add a new row from that month using the outstanding amount, new rate and months left from your bank statement, and clear the old row.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes. In Google Sheets choose File, Import, Upload and pick the file. The formulas, drop-downs and the Schedule tab keep working.
